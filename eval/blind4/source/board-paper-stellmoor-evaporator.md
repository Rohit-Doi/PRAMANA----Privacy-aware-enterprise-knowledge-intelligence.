# Board Paper BP-2026-31 — Replacement of Evaporator No. 2, Stellmoor Creamery

**Ombrellick Growers' Cooperative Society**
**Paper dated 6 August 2026 — for decision at the Board's fourth ordinary meeting of 2026**
**Sponsor: Plant Manager, Stellmoor Creamery. Executive owner: Regional Director, Dunmarrow Lowlands**
**Classification: CONFIDENTIAL — Board paper**

## 1. Decision sought

1.1 The Board is asked to approve capital expenditure of $4,612,000 to replace Evaporator No. 2 at the Stellmoor Creamery with a mechanical vapour recompression unit (Option C below).

1.2 Board approval is needed because the commitment is above the limit of delegation tier D5 in the Procurement and Purchasing Policy, the highest tier held by any executive.

1.3 The Capital Works Committee considered the proposal at its second meeting of 2026 and recommends Option C.

## 2. Background

2.1 Evaporator No. 2 was installed in 1998 and concentrates milk ahead of the powder dryer. It handles about 58% of the creamery's milk powder throughput.

2.2 In the 2025-26 flush season the unit had 11 unplanned stoppages, causing 143 hours of lost production. On two occasions milk had to be diverted to a third-party processor at a cost of $96,700.

2.3 The insurer's engineering survey of March 2026 rated the calandria tubes as "end of life" and gave the unit an expected remaining life of no more than two seasons.

## 3. Options considered

| Option | Description | Capital cost | Annual operating effect | Assessment |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Re-tube and refurbish the existing unit | $1,870,000 | No energy saving | Buys about 6 years; capacity unchanged |
| B | Replace like for like (thermal vapour recompression) | $3,945,000 | Energy saving of $171,000 a year | Capacity unchanged |
| C | Replace with mechanical vapour recompression unit | $4,612,000 | Energy saving of $538,000 a year | Recommended; capacity rises |
| D | Close the evaporator and contract out concentration | Nil | Extra cost of $1,260,000 a year | Loss of control over quality |
| E | Defer for one season | Nil | Expected stoppage cost of $310,000 | High risk of failure in flush season |

## 4. The recommended option

4.1 Option C raises evaporation capacity from 38.0 to 46.5 tonnes of water removed per hour.

4.2 The capital cost of $4,612,000 is made up of a supply-and-install contract of $4,180,000 and a contingency of $432,000.

4.3 With an energy saving of $538,000 a year against a capital cost of $4,612,000, the simple payback period is about 8.6 years. Against Option B the extra capital is $667,000 and the extra saving is $367,000 a year, so the additional spend pays back in under 2 years.

4.4 Steam demand at the creamery would fall by an estimated 31%, which also reduces the load on Boiler 1.

## 5. Procurement

5.1 Because the value is above the open tender threshold, the supply-and-install contract will go to open tender under the current Procurement and Purchasing Policy, for the minimum open period the policy requires.

5.2 Three manufacturers have confirmed interest. The panel will be chaired by the Head of Procurement.

## 6. Programme

| Milestone | Target date |

| Contract award | 30 November 2026 |
| Equipment delivered to site | 3 May 2027 |

6.1 The 19-day shutdown is timed for the low season, when daily milk intake is below 40% of peak and Evaporator No. 1 can carry the load.

## 7. Funding

7.1 The amount of $4,612,000 is included in the 2026-27 capital programme, subject to this approval.

7.2 An energy-efficiency grant application for $385,000 has been lodged with the Dunmarrow Rural Development Agency. The outcome is expected in November 2026 and is not assumed in this paper.

## 8. Risks

8.1 Delivery delay: the contract will carry liquidated damages of $11,800 for each day of delay beyond the delivery date, capped at 8% of the contract value.

8.2 Electrical supply: the new unit needs a 1.9 megavolt-ampere supply upgrade, quoted at $214,000 and included in the supply-and-install figure.

8.3 Food safety: the Head of Food Safety and Quality must validate the cleaning cycle before product is released from the new unit.

## 9. Recommendation

That the Board approves Option C at a capital cost of $4,612,000 and authorises the Chief Executive to sign the contract after the tender, provided the tendered price is within the approved amount.
