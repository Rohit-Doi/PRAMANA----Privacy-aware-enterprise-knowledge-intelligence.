# Regulatory Enforcement File — Pellam Bay Retort Deviation

**Ombrellick Growers' Cooperative Society — Legal**
**File reference LEG-ENF-2026-03 — position as at 12 June 2026**
**Classification: RESTRICTED — legally privileged. Prepared for the purpose of obtaining legal advice. Do not copy.**

## 1. Summary

1.1 The Kirrabel Coast Food Standards Inspectorate ("the Inspectorate") has issued Enforcement Notice EN-2026-0417 against the Society. The notice alleges that a batch of canned butter beans left the Pellam Bay Cannery without receiving the scheduled thermal process.

1.2 The Inspectorate proposes a penalty of $186,300. The Society has made a without-prejudice settlement offer and the matter is listed for hearing if no settlement is reached.

## 2. Facts

2.1 On the night shift of 6 May 2026, Retort 4 on Line 2 recorded a steam supply drop lasting 7 minutes during the sterilisation of lot PB-26-1139 (butter beans, 410 g cans).

2.2 The scheduled process for the product is 121.1 °C for 24 minutes. The chart shows the retort held at or above the scheduled temperature for only 17 minutes.

2.3 The deviation was not noticed by the retort operator and the lot of 18,420 cans was released on 8 May 2026. The chart review that should happen before release was signed without the chart being examined.

2.4 The deviation was found on 26 May 2026 during a routine records audit by the Food Safety and Quality team. The Society notified the Inspectorate the next day and published a recall notice on 29 May 2026.

2.5 No illness has been reported. Laboratory incubation of 240 retained cans showed no spoilage.

## 4. The notice and the Society's response

4.1 The Society must respond formally to the amended notice within 34 days of service.

4.2 The Inspectorate's penalty guidelines allow a reduction of up to 35% for self-reporting and prompt recall. Applying the full reduction to the proposed penalty of $186,300 gives $121,095, which is the amount of the Society's settlement offer.

4.3 If there is no settlement, the matter will be heard by the Coastal Regulatory Tribunal on 17 November 2026.

## 6. Legal assessment

6.1 Liability for the release of under-processed product is difficult to contest. The Society's best arguments go to the size of the penalty.

6.2 External counsel's view is that the Tribunal would be unlikely to impose more than the proposed penalty, and that a settlement at or near the offer has a better than even chance of being accepted.

6.3 The maximum penalty the Tribunal could impose for this category of breach is $415,000.

## 7. Related matters

7.1 A separate People team investigation concerns the conduct of a supervisor at Pellam Bay. It is not about the retort deviation and must not be confused with it.

7.2 The retort controls upgrade in the 2026-27 capital programme will add automatic deviation alarms and a hold on release until the chart is electronically reviewed.

7.3 The matter must not be discussed with the Inspectorate by anyone other than the General Counsel or external counsel. Site staff who are approached by an inspector should give factual records only and refer questions to the General Counsel.

## 8. Next steps

8.1 The General Counsel will report the Inspectorate's reply to the settlement offer to the Audit and Risk Committee at the first meeting that follows the reply.

8.2 Witness statements from the retort operator, the shift supervisor and the Head of Food Safety and Quality are to be finalised by 31 July 2026.
