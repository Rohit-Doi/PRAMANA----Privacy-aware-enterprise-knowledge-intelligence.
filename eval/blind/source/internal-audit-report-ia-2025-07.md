# Internal Audit Report IA-2025-07: Purchasing from Research Budgets

**Halvorsen Institute of Technology — Internal Audit and Compliance**
**Classification:** CONFIDENTIAL — Finance and Audit Committee, President, Chief Financial Officer, Dean of Research
**Issued:** 6 November 2025 | **Lead auditor:** Zoltan Whitcombe, Head of Internal Audit

## 1. Objective and scope

1.1 The audit examined whether purchases charged to research budgets complied with the Research Grants and Procurement Approval Policy (RES-POL-11).

1.2 The period reviewed was 1 July 2024 to 30 June 2025. Because RES-POL-11 took effect on 1 May 2025, purchases before that date were tested against the same thresholds, which were carried over unchanged from the earlier procedure.

1.3 The auditors drew a sample of 180 purchases with a combined value of $6.9 million from all four campuses. Fieldwork took place between 1 September and 10 October 2025.

## 2. Overall opinion

The overall rating is **Needs Improvement**, the third of four levels (Satisfactory, Adequate, Needs Improvement, Unsatisfactory). Controls are designed adequately but are not applied consistently.

## 3. Findings

| No. | Finding | Rating | Cases found | Value |
|-----|---------|--------|-------------|-------|
| F1 | Purchases above $40,000 placed without the approval that RES-POL-11 requires at that level | High | 14 | $1.12 million |
| F2 | Purchases above $10,000 supported by fewer than three written quotations | Medium | 23 | $0.61 million |
| F3 | Orders divided so that each part fell below an approval threshold | Medium | 9 | $0.34 million |
| F4 | Equipment costing more than $2,500 not tagged or entered in the asset register | Low | 31 | $0.27 million |
| F5 | Annual conflict-of-interest declaration missing for the person requesting the purchase | Medium | 6 of 40 tested | Not applicable |

## 4. Detail on the high-rated finding

4.1 In all 14 cases under F1 the order had been approved by a Head of Department only. In 11 of them the approval required at that level was obtained afterwards, between 3 and 47 days after the order was placed. In the remaining three it was never obtained.

4.2 Nine of the 14 cases arose in the two months after Prof. Emeric Thackeray-Sousa retired, when requests were being routed to an unattended mailbox.

4.3 The auditors found no evidence of loss or of personal gain in any of the 14 cases.

## 6. Recommendations and management response

6.1 **F1.** The purchasing system should refuse to release an order above $40,000 until the required approval is recorded. *Response of the Head of Procurement:* agreed; the system change will be in place by 31 January 2026.

6.2 **F2 and F3.** The Procurement Office should run a monthly report of orders to the same supplier within 30 days and review any pattern. *Response:* agreed; first report by 15 December 2025.

6.3 **F4.** The Research Office should complete tagging of the 31 items. *Response of the Dean of Research:* agreed; to be completed by 28 November 2025.

6.4 **F5.** Declarations should be collected through the HR portal each September. *Response of the Director of Human Resources:* agreed, from September 2026.

## 7. Follow-up

Internal Audit will test the implementation of these actions in April 2026 and report to the Finance and Audit Committee at its June 2026 meeting.
