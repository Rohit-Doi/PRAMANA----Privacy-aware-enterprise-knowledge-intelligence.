# Examination Regulations 2026

**Halvorsen Institute of Technology — Office of the Controller of Examinations**
**Document reference:** ER-2026 | **Effective:** 12 January 2026 | **Classification:** Internal

These regulations replace the Examination Regulations 2024 (ER-2024) in full. From the effective date ER-2024 has no force except as preserved by the transitional provisions in section 14.

## 1. Scope and definitions

1.1 These regulations govern all taught undergraduate and postgraduate examinations at the Brackenridge Campus and at the Toronto, Lisbon and Pune campuses, whether held in person or under remote supervision.

1.2 They do not apply to the examination of doctoral theses.

1.3 In these regulations "the Controller" means the Controller of Examinations; "working day" means Monday to Friday, excluding days on which the Institute is closed; "paper" means a single final examination for one course.

## 2. Eligibility to sit an examination

2.1 A student must have attended at least 80% of the scheduled teaching sessions of a course in order to sit its final examination.

2.2 Where a student's attendance is below 80% but not below 70%, the Dean of Academic Affairs may condone the shortfall on documented medical grounds. No condonation is possible below 70%.

2.3 Attendance is taken from the electronic register. A student who believes the register is wrong must raise the matter with the course leader before the last day of teaching; later corrections are not accepted.

2.4 A student whose fees are in arrears on the first day of the examination period may sit the examination, but the result will be withheld until the account is cleared.

## 3. Assessment structure

3.1 Unless a course outline approved by the Dean of Academic Affairs states otherwise, continuous assessment and the final examination each carry 50% of the course mark.

3.2 The overall pass mark for a course is 45%. In addition, a student must obtain at least 40% in the final examination component.

3.3 Continuous assessment marks must be released to students no later than five working days before the start of the examination period.

## 4. Grade boundaries

| Grade | Mark range (%) | Grade points |
|-------|----------------|--------------|
| A+ | 90–100 | 4.0 |
| A | 80–89 | 3.7 |
| B | 68–79 | 3.0 |
| C | 56–67 | 2.0 |
| D | 45–55 | 1.0 |
| F | 0–44 | 0.0 |

4.1 Marks are rounded to the nearest whole number before a grade is assigned. No other adjustment at a grade boundary is permitted.

## 5. Examination venues

5.1 The following venues on the Brackenridge Campus are approved for written examinations. The examination capacity is lower than the ordinary seating capacity because alternate seats are left empty.

| Venue | Ordinary capacity | Examination capacity |
|-------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Calder Examination Centre, Hall A | 240 | 240 |
| Calder Examination Centre, Hall B | 180 | 180 |
| Marlowe Auditorium | 620 | 310 |
| Okonkwo-Reyes Engineering Hall, Lab 3 (computer-based papers) | 64 | 64 |

5.2 Regional campuses designate their own venues, which must be approved by the Controller at least 30 days before the examination period.

## 6. Conduct of examinations

6.1 Candidates must be seated fifteen minutes before the published start time and must show a valid student identity card.

6.2 A candidate who arrives late is admitted up to 20 minutes after the start. No extra time is given. No candidate may leave the room during the first 45 minutes or the last 15 minutes of a paper.

6.3 Mobile phones, smart watches, earphones and any device capable of storing or receiving text are not permitted at the desk. They must be switched off and placed in the sealed pouch provided at the door.

6.4 One invigilator is appointed for every 40 candidates, with a minimum of two invigilators in any room.

6.5 Candidates with an approved accommodation receive 25% additional time. Applications must reach the Disability Support Office at least six weeks before the first day of the examination period.

## 7. Remote supervision

7.1 A paper may be taken under remote supervision only by students registered at a regional campus, and only with the Controller's written approval.

7.2 The candidate's identity is verified by camera against the student identity card before the paper is released. A break in the video connection of more than five minutes in total voids the attempt, and the candidate is entered for the next supplementary window without a fee.

## 8. Academic malpractice

8.1 Malpractice is dealt with according to the following levels.

| Level | Example | Standard penalty |
|-------|---------|------------------|
| 1 | Prohibited device at the desk, not used | Written warning; paper stands |
| 2 | Unauthorised notes or use of a device | Mark of zero for the paper |
| 3 | Impersonation or prior access to the paper | Mark of zero for all papers in the period and referral to the Student Discipline Panel |

8.2 The invigilator records the incident on Form EX-7 before the candidate leaves the room. The Controller decides Level 1 and Level 2 cases within ten working days.

## 9. Publication of results

9.1 Results are published on the student portal within 15 working days of the last day of the examination period.

9.2 Results are not given by telephone or e-mail.

## 10. Resit and supplementary examinations

10.1 A student who fails a course may resit the final examination. A course may be attempted up to two times in total, including the first attempt. A student who fails the second attempt must repeat the course with attendance.

10.2 The resit fee is $75 per paper.

10.3 The grade awarded after a successful resit is capped at C.

10.4 Resits and deferred papers are held only in the supplementary examination window published in the academic calendar for the semester concerned. No individual sittings are arranged outside that window.

10.5 A student who misses a paper because of illness must submit a medical certificate to the Registrar's Office within three working days of the paper. If the absence is accepted, the supplementary sitting counts as the same attempt and no fee is charged.

## 11. Re-evaluation of scripts

11.1 A student may apply for re-evaluation of a final examination script within 10 working days of the publication of results.

11.2 The re-evaluation fee is $50 per paper. The fee is refunded if the mark changes by four marks or more in either direction.

11.3 Re-evaluation is carried out by an examiner who did not mark the original script and who is not told the original mark. The re-evaluated mark replaces the original mark, whether it is higher or lower.

11.4 The outcome is communicated within 15 working days of the application.

## 12. Appeals

12.1 A student who remains dissatisfied after re-evaluation may appeal to the Examination Appeals Board within seven working days of receiving the re-evaluation outcome. An appeal may be made only on the grounds of procedural irregularity or of circumstances the student could not reasonably have disclosed earlier; disagreement with academic judgement is not a ground.

12.2 The Board has five members. Three members, including the chair, form a quorum.

12.3 The Board will issue a written decision within 20 working days of receiving a complete appeal. The decision of the Board is final within the Institute.

## 13. Custody of scripts and papers

13.1 Sealed question papers are stored in the examination paper vault in the Calder Examination Centre and are released to the chief invigilator no earlier than 45 minutes before the start of the paper.

13.2 Marked scripts are retained for 18 months after the publication of results and are then destroyed under the supervision of the Controller.

## 14. Transitional provisions

14.1 For a course that a student first attempted before 12 January 2026, the pass mark of 40% and the limit of three attempts under ER-2024 continue to apply to that student and that course until 31 December 2026.

14.2 Applications for re-evaluation of results published before 12 January 2026 are handled under ER-2024, including its fee.

14.3 All other provisions of these regulations apply from the effective date to every student.
