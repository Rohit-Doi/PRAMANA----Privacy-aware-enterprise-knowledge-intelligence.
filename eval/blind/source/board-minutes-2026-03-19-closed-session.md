## 4. Project Heron — proposed acquisition of Calloway College of Applied Sciences

4.1 The President presented the proposal. Calloway College of Applied Sciences is a private college in Greywater Bay with 1,150 students and 84 staff. Its trustees approached the Institute in October 2025 after two years of operating deficits. Under the proposal the Institute would acquire the college's campus, buildings and teaching licences, and the college would become the Institute's fourth regional campus under the name "Halvorsen Institute — Calloway Campus".

4.2 The Chief Financial Officer set out the financial terms.

| Term | Agreed position |
|------|-----------------|
| Purchase price | $38.5 million |
| Paid from the Capital Reserve Fund | 60% of the price ($23.1 million) |
| Paid from a new term loan | 40% of the price ($15.4 million) |
| Lender | Northmere Cooperative Bank |
| Loan term and rate | 12 years at a fixed rate of 5.4% |
| Break fee payable by the Institute if it withdraws | $1.2 million |
| Exclusivity period | Until 15 May 2026 |
| Target completion date | 1 September 2026 |

4.3 He reported that the college's buildings had been independently valued at $44.0 million and that deferred maintenance was estimated at $6.2 million over the first five years. On the Institute's projections the Calloway Campus would reach break-even in its third full year of operation.

4.4 The General Counsel reported on due diligence, which is being carried out by Ashdown Pryce LLP for a fee capped at $420,000. Two matters remain open: a boundary dispute affecting the college's sports field, and the transfer of one teaching licence, which requires the consent of the regional accreditation body. She advised that completion should be made conditional on both.

4.5 In discussion the following points were made.

- Ms. Novakovic-Hale said the Finance and Audit Committee had reviewed the financing on 5 March 2026 and supported it, provided that the Capital Reserve Fund is rebuilt to at least $25 million within four years.
- Dr. Mbeki-Lund asked what would happen to Calloway's staff. The President replied that all 84 staff would transfer on their existing terms and that their employment would be guaranteed for 24 months from completion.
- Ms. Eisenhardt and Mr. Whitlock-Araya considered the price too high in view of the deferred maintenance and said they would prefer a price no higher than $34 million.
- Prof. Dubrovnik asked about current Calloway students. The President confirmed that they would complete their programmes at the tuition rates they had been promised, and that Institute fee levels would apply only to students admitted from the 2027 intake onward.

4.6 **Resolved**, by nine votes to two with one abstention, that the Board approves the acquisition on the terms set out in paragraph 4.2, subject to satisfactory completion of due diligence and the two conditions in paragraph 4.4, and authorises the President and the Chief Financial Officer to sign the purchase agreement. Ms. Eisenhardt and Mr. Whitlock-Araya voted against. Mr. Thornbury abstained.

4.7 **Confidentiality.** The Board agreed that the project remains under embargo until 30 June 2026 or until the purchase agreement is signed, whichever is earlier. Until then the project is to be referred to only as "Project Heron" in any written communication, and knowledge of it is limited to governors, the President, the Provost, the Chief Financial Officer, the General Counsel and the Secretary to the Board. A joint announcement with the Calloway trustees will be prepared by the President's office.

## 5. Tuition for the 2026–27 academic year

5.1 The Chief Financial Officer proposed an increase of 4.2% in all tuition rates for 2026–27, compared with the 3.8% applied for 2025–26. Residence band fees would rise by 3.0%. The application fee and the registration fee would not change.

5.2 Ms. Castellano-Reid asked that the scholarship budget be increased in step. The Chief Financial Officer confirmed that it would rise by the same 4.2%.

5.3 **Resolved** unanimously that the proposed rates are approved. The new fee schedule is to be published by 1 May 2026.

## 6. Renewal of the President's appointment

6.1 The President, the Chief Financial Officer and the General Counsel left the room for this item.

6.2 Mr. Abernathy-Osei reported the recommendation of the Remuneration Committee, made at its meeting of 12 February 2026, that Dr. Oyelaran-Finch be reappointed for a further term of four years from 1 July 2026 on the terms set out in the Executive Remuneration Schedule.

6.3 **Resolved** unanimously, by the eleven governors present for the item, that the reappointment is approved.

## 7. Risk register

7.1 The Board noted the security incident of November 2025 affecting student records and the report of the Chief Information Security Officer. Mr. Halvorsen-Pike asked that the Board receive confirmation at its next meeting that multi-factor authentication is in place for every staff account.

7.2 The Board noted that a claim for wrongful dismissal has been brought against the Institute by a former member of academic staff and that mediation is scheduled for May 2026. The General Counsel will report the outcome.

## 8. Financial position

8.1 The Chief Financial Officer reported that the second-quarter reforecast shows an operating surplus of $1.9 million for the year, compared with $4.7 million in the approved budget, and that spending controls have been in force since 1 March 2026.

8.2 Ms. Rowntree asked whether the acquisition approved under item 4 was prudent in a year when the surplus had fallen. The Chief Financial Officer replied that the shortfall arises from hall occupancy, the timing of research income and energy costs, none of which affects the Capital Reserve Fund, and that tuition income remains on budget.

8.3 The Board noted the report. It asked that the third-quarter reforecast be circulated to all governors as soon as the Finance and Audit Committee has seen it, without waiting for the June meeting.
