## 2. Project Lantern — restructuring of fares

2.1 Mr. Peverell presented paper CS/26/04. Project Lantern would replace the present flat fares with a distance-based structure of three zones for bus and light rail travel. Ferry fares would stay outside the zone structure for the time being.

2.2 The proposed adult single fares, paid by TapLine card, are $2.20 for a journey within one zone, $3.40 for a journey across two zones and $4.60 for a journey across all three zones. The cash premium on buses would remain.

2.3 The 7-day pass would be withdrawn and replaced by a weekly cap of $27.00. The 30-day pass would be kept, at a price still to be decided.

2.4 The paper proposed reducing the concession discount from 50% of the adult fare to 40%.

2.5 Mr. Peverell reported the results of the modelling. Fare revenue would rise by about $6.8 million a year, an increase of 7.4%. Ridership would fall by an estimated 1.9% in the first year. Of present journeys, 52% would cost less than today, 31% would cost more and 17% would cost the same. Journeys that cost more are concentrated on the outer ends of Routes 3 and 8 and on end-to-end Copperline trips.

2.6 The proposed start date is Monday 4 January 2027. To meet it, a public consultation of eight weeks would need to open on Monday 7 September 2026. Nothing is to be said publicly before that date. Reprogramming of the TapLine validators would take 14 weeks and is estimated to cost $1.9 million.

2.7 In discussion:

- Ms. Fenn said that the reduction in the concession discount would fall hardest on older passengers and students and that she could not support the package while it included that element.
- Mr. Okoro agreed and asked for the revenue effect of the concession change to be shown separately. Mr. Peverell said it accounted for $1.3 million of the $6.8 million.
- Mr. Starkweather said that the zone structure was sound and overdue, and that the Authority's fare revenue per boarding had fallen in real terms for four years.
- Ms. Roanoke asked whether the consultation could be genuine if the Board had already approved the scheme. The General Counsel advised that approval in principle, with the final decision reserved until after consultation, was the proper course.
- Mr. Harrowgate asked that front-line staff be briefed at least two weeks before the consultation opens so that they are not surprised by passengers' questions.

2.8 **Resolved**, by five votes to two (Ms. Fenn and Mr. Okoro against):

- (a) that Project Lantern be approved in principle as the basis for public consultation;
- (b) that the Director of Fares and Commercial bring alternatives to the reduction in the concession discount to the Board's June ordinary meeting, including one option that leaves the discount at 50%;
- (c) that the final decision be taken at the Board's last ordinary meeting of 2026, after the consultation has closed;
- (d) that the project remain under embargo until the consultation opens.

Mr. Peverell left the meeting.

## 3. Project Heron — proposed acquisition of a private operator

3.1 The Chief Executive presented paper CS/26/05. Project Heron is the proposed purchase of Tidewater Shuttle Lines Ltd, a privately owned company that runs harbour shuttle ferries and two water taxis from Quillon Pier and from its own jetty at Saltmarsh Steps.

3.2 Tidewater has three passenger vessels (MV Saltmarsh, MV Curlew and MV Osprey Bay), 61 employees and annual revenue of about $9.3 million. Its owners approached the Authority in January 2026 and have indicated that they would accept a price in the region of $14.2 million.

3.3 The Chief Executive set out the case. The purchase would give the Authority three shallow-draught vessels, relieving its dependence on MV Marlin, and would remove a competitor that takes an estimated 380,000 passengers a year on the Garrow Point corridor. She told the Board that another operator, Northmere Coachways, is understood to have expressed interest in Tidewater.

3.4 The Chief Financial Officer explained the proposed financing: $9.0 million from the capital reserve and the remainder from a seven-year loan. He said that the capital reserve could bear this without breaching the reserves policy, but that it would leave little room for unplanned capital spending in financial year 2027.

3.5 Halewood Carrick Advisory has been engaged as financial adviser. The budget for due diligence, covering the vessels' condition, employment liabilities and Tidewater's jetty lease, is $420,000.

3.6 In discussion:

- Dr. Kessington asked about the staff. The Chief Executive said that all 61 employees would transfer and that the Authority would undertake to keep their existing terms and conditions for at least 24 months.
- Mr. Harrowgate asked for an independent survey of all three vessels before any binding offer. This was agreed.
- Ms. Roanoke asked that the Audit and Risk Committee see the due diligence report before the Board is asked for a final decision.

3.7 **Resolved**, unanimously:

- (a) that the Chief Executive be authorised to make a non-binding offer for Tidewater Shuttle Lines Ltd of up to $15.5 million;
- (b) that the due diligence budget of $420,000 be approved;
- (c) that the Chief Executive seek exclusivity from the owners until 31 July 2026;
- (d) that no binding offer be made without a further resolution of the Board;
- (e) that the target date for completion, if the Board proceeds, be 1 December 2026.

## 4. Litigation — claim LC-2026-007

4.1 The General Counsel reported on the personal injury claim arising from the light rail collision of November 2025. The claim is for $410,000. Outside counsel has advised that an early settlement is in the Authority's interest.

4.2 The General Counsel's standing authority to settle claims is $150,000. He asked the Board to raise it for this claim only.

4.3 **Resolved**, unanimously, that the General Counsel be authorised to settle claim LC-2026-007 for any sum up to $275,000 without further reference, and that any proposed settlement above that figure be brought to the Finance and Performance Committee.
