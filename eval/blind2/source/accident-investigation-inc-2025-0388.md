# Accident Investigation File INC-2025-0388

**Brennholt Regional Transit Authority — Safety and Security**
**Subject:** Collision between a Copperline light rail vehicle and a road vehicle at Weaver Street level crossing
**Final report dated:** 16 January 2026
**Investigation lead:** Head of Safety and Security
**Investigator:** Mr. Corwin Aldahl, Senior Safety Investigator
**Classification:** RESTRICTED — contains witness evidence and material subject to legal privilege. Distribution limited to the Chief Executive, the General Counsel, the Director of Operations, the Director of Engineering and the Chair of the Safety Committee.

## 1. Summary

1.1 At 07:42 on Tuesday 18 November 2025, Copperline vehicle 2107, working an outbound service from Brennholt Central towards Lindenmoor, struck a delivery van on the Weaver Street level crossing, between the Tanners Row and Weaver Street stops.

1.2 The light rail vehicle was carrying 64 passengers. Nine people were injured: eight passengers and the driver of the van. Two of the injured were taken to hospital, one passenger with a fractured wrist and a shoulder injury, and the van driver with chest injuries. Both were discharged within three days. Nobody was killed.

1.3 The light rail vehicle did not derail. Its leading section was badly damaged. The van, operated by Brisk Parcel Couriers, was written off.

1.4 The immediate cause was that the van entered the crossing against the warning signals. Two conditions on the Authority's side contributed: a warning lamp on the northern road approach was not working, and overgrown vegetation reduced the light rail operator's view of that approach.

## 2. Sequence of events

2.1 The service left Brennholt Central at 07:31, two minutes late. It left Tanners Row at 07:41.

2.2 The event recorder shows that the vehicle approached the crossing at 29 km/h. The permitted speed over the crossing is 30 km/h.

2.3 The forward-facing camera shows the van entering the crossing from the northern approach without slowing. The operator applied the emergency brake 1.8 seconds before impact. The speed at impact was 23 km/h.

2.4 The vehicle came to a stand 11 metres beyond the point of impact. The operator made an emergency call to the Copperline control room at 07:43.

2.5 The emergency services arrived at 07:51. Passengers who could walk were led off by the operator and a duty controller between 07:55 and 08:10.

2.6 The Authority notified the State Rail Safety Inspectorate at 09:20, within the two hours required.

2.7 The Copperline was closed between Brennholt Central and Lindenmoor for 5 hours and 10 minutes. Trams continued to run between Ashgrove and Brennholt Central. Fourteen replacement buses from Stanmere Depot covered the closed section. The line reopened fully at 12:52.

## 3. The operator

3.1 The operator, referred to in this report as Operator A, is a light rail operator at grade OP3, based at Ashgrove Light Rail Depot, with six years' service on the Copperline and no previous safety incidents.

3.2 Tests for alcohol and drugs taken at 09:05 were negative.

3.3 The day of the collision was Operator A's sixth consecutive working day. In the week of the collision Operator A had already worked 11.5 hours of overtime, which had been approved in writing by the General Manager, Central Division. The rest period before the shift was 10 hours and 20 minutes.

3.4 All of these figures were within the limits of the Overtime and Fatigue Management Policy in force on the day. The investigator nevertheless found that the limits themselves allowed too little margin, and that Operator A's reaction time, while not unreasonable, was at the slower end of the range seen in simulator assessments.

3.5 Operator A was not at fault. No disciplinary action is recommended.

## 4. The crossing

4.1 Weaver Street is an open crossing protected by flashing warning lamps and an audible alarm on each road approach. It has no barriers.

4.2 The lamp unit on the northern approach was reported as failed by a Copperline operator on Friday 14 November 2025, four days before the collision. Work order WO-58812 was raised the same day.

4.3 The Authority's standard requires a safety-critical signalling fault to be repaired within 24 hours. The work order was wrongly coded as "routine", which carries a repair target of 10 working days. The audible alarm on the northern approach was working throughout.

4.4 Vegetation on the embankment north of the crossing had last been cut back in May 2025. At the time of the collision it reduced the operator's view of the northern approach from the 120 metres required by the standard to about 70 metres.

4.5 Average daily road traffic over the crossing is about 3,400 vehicles. There had been two near-miss reports at the crossing in the previous 12 months.

## 5. The road vehicle

5.1 The van driver told investigators that he did not see any warning lamps and did not hear the alarm because the van's radio was on. He had been on duty since 04:30.

5.2 The police investigation into the van driver is separate from this report and had not concluded at the date of the final report.

## 6. Damage and cost

6.1 Repairs to vehicle 2107 cost $186,000. The vehicle was out of service for 47 days and returned to passenger service on 4 January 2026.

6.2 Repairs to the crossing surface, lamps and a signalling cabinet cost $23,500.

6.3 Replacement buses and staff overtime on the day cost $9,800.

## 7. Findings

- F1. The immediate cause was the road vehicle entering the crossing against the warning signals.
- F2. The failed warning lamp on the northern approach was a contributory factor. It should have been repaired by 15 November.
- F3. The fault-coding process allowed a safety-critical fault to be treated as routine without any second check.
- F4. The vegetation inspection interval of 12 months is too long for crossings with restricted sightlines.
- F5. The working-time limits in force allowed an operator to be on a sixth consecutive day with a rest period only 20 minutes above the minimum.
- F6. The response of the operator, the control room and the duty controller after the collision was prompt and correct.

## 8. Recommendations

Interim recommendations R4 and R5 were issued on 12 December 2025 so that they could be taken into account in the policy revision then under way. The full set is as follows.

| No. | Recommendation | Owner | Deadline |
|---|---|---|---|
| R1 | Repair and test all warning lamps at the five open crossings on the Copperline | Director of Engineering | Completed 21 November 2025 |
| R2 | Change the fault-coding system so that any fault on crossing equipment is coded safety-critical by default and can only be downgraded by a signalling engineer | Director of Engineering | 27 February 2026 |
| R3 | Inspect vegetation at all open crossings every 3 months and cut back to restore the full 120-metre sightline | Director of Engineering | First cycle by 31 March 2026 |
| R4 | Raise the minimum rest period between shifts to 11 hours | Director of People | 1 February 2026 |
| R5 | Reduce the maximum number of consecutive working days and require approval at director level for overtime above the weekly limit | Director of People | 1 February 2026 |
| R6 | Assess Weaver Street and Cooper's Wharf crossings for half-barriers and report the cost to the Safety Committee | Director of Engineering | Safety Committee meeting of June 2026 |
| R7 | Brief all Copperline operators on the findings of this report | General Manager, Central Division | 13 February 2026 |

## 9. Evidence held on file

9.1 The file holds the event recorder download, forward and saloon camera footage from vehicle 2107, crossing equipment logs, work order WO-58812, the control room voice recording, Operator A's roster and overtime records for the preceding 13 weeks, and statements from 17 witnesses.

9.2 Camera footage is kept beyond the normal retention period because a claim has been notified. It must not be released without the consent of the General Counsel.

## 10. Sign-off

The final report was accepted by the Head of Safety and Security on 16 January 2026 and presented to the Safety Committee at its February 2026 meeting. Progress against the recommendations is reported to that committee at each meeting until all are closed.
