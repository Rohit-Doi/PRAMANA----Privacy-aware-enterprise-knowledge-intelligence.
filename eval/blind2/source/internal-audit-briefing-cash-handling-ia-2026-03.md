# Internal Audit Briefing: Cash Handling and Ticket Office Controls

Audit reference IA-2026-03. Briefing for the Audit and Risk Committee, prepared 21 April 2026 by the Head of Internal Audit and Compliance. Lead auditor: Ms. Junia Halvorsen. Classification: CONFIDENTIAL.

## Scope of the audit

- Fieldwork ran from 9 March to 27 March 2026.
- Three sites were visited: the Quillon Pier ticket office, the Brennholt Central Station ticket office and the cash room at Marrow Lane Depot.
- The audit tested how cash from ticket sales and on-board fares is counted, reconciled, stored and banked, and how refunds are approved.
- The review period was the six months from 1 September 2025 to 28 February 2026.
- TapLine card payments and online sales were outside the scope.

## Overall opinion

- The opinion is "partial assurance", the third of the four levels used by Internal Audit. The level below it is "no assurance".
- There are nine findings: three rated high, four rated medium and two rated low.
- Controls at Brennholt Central were largely sound. Most of the high-rated findings relate to Quillon Pier.
- No evidence of fraud was found, but the weaknesses would make fraud hard to detect.

## High-rated findings

- Unexplained cash shortages at Quillon Pier totalled $11,740 over the six-month review period. Shortages were written off each week by the ticket office supervisor with no review by anyone more senior.
- Of 120 end-of-shift reconciliations sampled across the three sites, 38 had no second signature. The cash handling procedure requires two signatures on every reconciliation.
- The safe combination at Quillon Pier had not been changed for 26 months. The procedure requires a change every 6 months and whenever a key holder leaves. Two former key holders had left during that time.

## Medium and low findings

- Refunds above $50 need a supervisor's approval. In a sample of 85 refunds above that amount, 17 had been paid without it.
- At Marrow Lane, cash was held overnight above the permitted limit on 9 of the 40 nights tested.
- Banking at Quillon Pier was done twice a week instead of daily during January 2026 because of staff shortages.
- The camera covering the counting desk at Marrow Lane had been out of order since 12 December 2025.
- Low-rated findings concern out-of-date float registers and missing training records for four ticket office clerks.

## Management response

- The General Manager, Harbour Division has accepted all findings relating to Quillon Pier. The safe combination was changed on 30 March 2026.
- Weekly shortages above $100 at any ticket office will now be reviewed by the Division's finance business partner.
- Written responses to every finding, with an owner and a date, are due ten working days before the Committee's second meeting of 2026.
- The Chief Financial Officer has asked that reconciliation sign-off be moved to an electronic form by 30 September 2026, so that a missing second signature blocks the banking entry.

## Follow-up

- Internal Audit will re-test all high-rated findings in October 2026 and report the result to the Committee's last meeting of the year.
- A similar audit of on-board cash handling on ferries is proposed for the financial year 2027 audit plan.
- The Committee is asked to note the opinion and to agree the follow-up timetable.
