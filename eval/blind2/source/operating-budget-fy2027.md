# Operating Budget and Capital Programme, Financial Year 2027

**Brennholt Regional Transit Authority — Finance Directorate**
**Reference:** FIN-BUD-FY27
**Period covered:** 1 July 2026 to 30 June 2027
**Adopted by the Board:** 25 June 2026
**Prepared by:** Chief Financial Officer
**Classification:** CONFIDENTIAL — the summary published with the Board papers omits sections 6, 8 and 9.

## 1. Overview

1.1 This document sets the Authority's operating budget and capital programme for financial year 2027. The operating budget is balanced: planned revenue and planned expenditure are both $318.4 million.

1.2 The budget for financial year 2026 was $306.9 million, so the increase is $11.5 million, or 3.7%. Most of the increase is pay, traction energy and the full-year cost of the frequency improvements made in April 2026.

1.3 The budget assumes no change to fares during the year beyond the tariff that took effect in March 2026.

## 2. Planning assumptions

2.1 Ridership is assumed to be 41.6 million boardings, 2.3% more than the forecast outturn for financial year 2026.

2.2 A pay award of 3.0% is assumed for staff on the operating grades from 1 January 2027. Each further 0.5% would cost about $0.5 million in the financial year.

2.3 Diesel is budgeted at $3.85 a gallon. The Authority has fixed the price of 60% of its expected diesel use through forward contracts at $3.70 a gallon.

2.4 Electricity for the Copperline and the electric bus fleet is budgeted at 14.2 cents per kilowatt-hour.

2.5 A vacancy factor of 4% has been applied to staff budgets in all divisions except for bus and light rail operators and ferry crew, where posts are budgeted in full.

2.6 General price inflation on non-pay budgets is assumed to be 2.6%.

## 8. Financial delegation bands

The bands below set who may approve a commitment to spend. They apply to operating and capital spending alike.

| Band | Value of commitment | Approver |
|---|---|---|
| A | Up to $10,000 | Depot manager or other named budget holder |
| B | $10,001 to $50,000 | Division General Manager or head of function |
| C | $50,001 to $250,000 | Director |
| D | $250,001 to $2,000,000 | Chief Financial Officer |
| E | Above $2,000,000 | Board, on the recommendation of the Finance and Performance Committee |

8.1 A commitment must not be split to bring it into a lower band.

8.2 A transfer of budget between divisions above $500,000 needs the approval of the Chief Financial Officer. Transfers between pay and non-pay budgets are not allowed without his approval, whatever the amount.

## 9. Reserves

9.1 The general reserve is forecast to stand at $21.4 million on 30 June 2026. The Authority's policy is to hold a general reserve of at least 6% of budgeted operating expenditure, which for financial year 2027 is $19.1 million. The reserve is therefore $2.3 million above the minimum.

9.2 The capital reserve is forecast to stand at $17.3 million on 30 June 2026, before the planned use of $7.5 million in section 7.

## 10. Risks

10.1 The main risks to the budget are, in order of size: a shortfall in the sales-tax levy; a pay settlement above 3.0%; overtime continuing at the level seen in financial year 2026; and claims costs above the insurance budget.

10.2 The budget makes no provision for any acquisition, for any change in fare structure, or for half-barriers at open level crossings. If any of these proceeds during the year, a supplementary budget will be brought to the Board.

10.3 The budget depends on a savings programme of $4.2 million, which is already deducted from the figures in sections 4 and 5. The three largest items are: $1.6 million from filling operator vacancies and so reducing overtime and agency use; $1.1 million from the new tyre and parts supply contract that starts on 1 August 2026; and $0.8 million from lower energy use once the depot lighting replacement is finished. The remaining $0.7 million is spread across head office budgets. Progress against the savings programme is reported with the quarterly figures, and a division that falls behind on its share must make up the difference from within its own budget.

10.4 Insurance premiums were renewed on 1 May 2026 at a cost 9% higher than the year before. The increase is included in the insurance and claims line.

## 11. Monitoring

11.1 Budget holders receive a monthly report by the tenth working day of the following month.

11.2 A division that forecasts an overspend of more than 1% of its budget must send a recovery plan to the Chief Financial Officer within 15 working days.

11.3 Quarterly reports go to the Finance and Performance Committee.

## 12. Adoption

The draft budget was considered by the Finance and Performance Committee on 14 May 2026 and adopted by the Board, without amendment, on 25 June 2026.
